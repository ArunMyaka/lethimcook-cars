06Suggested mods

  • Step 1 · Power

    Stage 1 ECU remap

    Recorded at 216 bhp / 451 Nm on a dyno, software only. Do it after the watch-outs are sorted, from a tuner who knows the N47.

  • Step 2 · Gearbox

    xHP gearbox tune

    TCU remap for the 8HP45: quicker shifts, raised torque limits, proper manual mode. The remap's other half.

  • Step 3 · Grip

    Tyres before anything else

    Ditch the hard run-flats for proper performance rubber (Michelin Pilot Sport class) in the stock size. Biggest single gain in ride, grip, steering feel and quiet.

  • Step 4 · Chassis

    Suspension refresh

    At this age the dampers are tired. Fresh quality dampers (Bilstein B6 class) or an M Sport setup bring back the tight, keyed-in feel.

  • Step 5 · Brakes

    340 mm front brake swap

    The 328i / 330d front setup bolts on: bigger discs, stronger bite. Or the full M Performance 370 mm kit if the wheels allow.

  • Step 6 · Looks

    Keep it OEM+

    M Sport bumpers, a subtle boot lip, 18-inch wheels in the factory style, LCI LED tail lamps — keeping the car close to BMW's own sportiest factory specification.

07Watch-outs

  • N47 timing chain

    The known weak point — the chain sits at the back of the engine, so replacement is an engine-out job. Listen for a rattle on cold start; a car with the chain done, with bills, is worth paying extra for.

  • DPF and short trips

    Diesel particulate filters clog on a life of short city runs. This car wants regular highway drives; if your use is 3 km hops, look at the petrol 328i instead.

  • Crankshaft damper pulley

    The rubber-bonded front pulley delaminates with age and heat. Squeal, wobble or rubber dust at the front of the engine means it's on its way out.

  • Gearbox oil — "lifetime" is a lie

    BMW sealed the 8HP45 "for life"; ZF recommends fresh Lifeguard 8 around 80,000–100,000 km. No record of a change = budget for one.

  • Run-flat ride

    Stock run-flats crash over sharp edges and cost plenty. Budget for the tyre swap in Step 3 and carry a puncture kit — there's no spare wheel.

  • Service history is everything

    Full BMW or good-specialist history, no warning lights, no "minor" leaks. At ₹6–7 lakh, the cheapest 320d is usually the most expensive one to own.

09The verdict

At ₹6–7 lakh, the 320d combines rear-wheel drive and strong diesel torque in a traditional sedan body — a combination that is rare at this price in India.
In brief
  • Rear-wheel drive, 50:50 balanceNo front-wheel-drive rival at this money drives like this
  • 380 Nm from 1,750 rpmFull torque on a light throttle — overtakes need no planning
  • 100 km/h at ≈1,500 rpm in 8th≈850 km from one 57-litre tank
  • The ZF 8-speedThe best automatic gearbox in its class, full stop
  • Hatchback moneyBadge buyers ignored the drive, so good cars sank to ₹6–7 lakh
  • Buttons and dials, not screensPhysical controls for climate and media, with a rotary iDrive controller
