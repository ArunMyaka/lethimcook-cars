03Suggested mods

  • Documented route

    Single-turbo conversion

    Removing the sequential twin-turbo system for one larger turbo is a well-documented tuning path in the FD scene. No power figure is quoted — results vary by build.

  • Reliability first

    Cooling upgrades

    Aluminium radiator and fan-switch upgrades are the documented first steps before any power work, because heat is the rotary's main enemy.

04Watch-outs

  • Apex seals

    The defining FD issue: apex and side-seal wear causes compression loss, accelerated by heat and oil starvation. A rotary-specific compression test is the standard pre-purchase check, and periodic engine refresh is a documented part of FD ownership.

  • Cooling runs marginal

    Stock cooling is marginal and overheating is a leading cause of seal failure. Documented reliability changes include an aluminium radiator, an upgraded fan switch and pre-cat removal.

  • The vacuum 'rat's nest'

    The sequential-turbo system runs through many small vacuum hoses and solenoids. Under-bonnet heat hardens them, causing boost-control faults; hose renewal is the documented fix.

  • It drinks oil on purpose

    Oil is metered into the chambers to lubricate the seals. Owners who treat it like a piston engine and skip level checks cause the failures above.

05The verdict

A 1,308 cc twin-rotor engine, sequential turbos and no pistons — the FD is the lightest, sharpest RX-7, built for just 68,589 buyers over eleven years.
